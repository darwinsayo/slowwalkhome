There's a shortcut home from the end of our road. It cuts behind the hardware store, past the dumpsters, and saves about four minutes. For years I took it without thinking, because four minutes is four minutes and there was always something waiting at the other end.

Lately I've been taking the long way. It bends past a field that nobody seems to own, a fence line that leans a little more every spring, and a maple that turns the color of a struck match sometime in mid-October.

I'd like to say I made this change on purpose — some wise decision about slowing down. The truth is I missed the turn one evening, and by the time I noticed, I was already halfway past the field. The light was low and gold. A pair of crows were arguing on the fence. I didn't hurry.

I think a lot of the good in my life has come that way: not by planning, but by missing the turn and deciding not to mind.
