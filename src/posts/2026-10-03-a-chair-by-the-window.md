Some mornings the words come easily. Most mornings they don't.

There's a chair by the window that nobody else in the house seems to want, which makes it mine by default. Before anyone is up, I sit there with coffee going cold and try to pray. Often what comes out is less a prayer than an inventory: the bills, the worries, the conversation I handled badly yesterday.

An old priest once told me that showing up is most of it — that sitting in the chair *is* the prayer, even on the days it feels like nothing happens. I didn't believe him for a long time. I'm starting to.

The window faces east. Whatever else happens, the light always arrives.
